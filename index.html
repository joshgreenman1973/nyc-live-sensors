The city that sensors see (and hear and feel)

Public feeds from the instruments watching New York City, read as they report.

Tide gauges, weather stations, stream gauges, a wave buoy, bridge-clearance sensors, traffic detectors, bike counters, weather radar, aircraft and ship transponders, bus and train trackers, bike docks, the power grid, a weather satellite and the seismographs underneath. Your browser reads most of them straight from the source; a small robot fetches the rest every 10 minutes. Connecting to the feeds.

About this page

  • This dashboard was built with AI assistance. Every number is fetched live from the public feed named under it — nothing is stored, modeled or adjusted by this page, with one labeled exception: the air-quality reading is a model estimate, not a physical sensor, and says so.
  • Feeds go down, lag or report bad values. Each reading shows its own "as of" time; a reading older than its feed's usual rhythm is marked lagging and gets a yellow rule, and an unreachable feed says no signal.
  • Readings here are for curiosity. For emergencies and safety decisions, rely on official channels.
  • Spot-check anything you plan to rely on. See the methodology for every endpoint, refresh cadence and known caveat.
